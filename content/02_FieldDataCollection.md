# Field Data Collection
While in the field, you can capture accurate, real-world information by recording the locations of features, such as soil samples, species observations, or transects, using your phone's GPS. You may already be familiar with collecting and contributing geographic data if you have reported an incident using Google Maps or uploaded an observation to iNaturalist.

Collecting accurate field data can present several challenges, including GPS accuracy and device quality. It is important to consider these factors before starting a project to ensure your device meets the requirements of your intended use. Additional challenges may arise from limited connectivity in remote locations, adverse weather conditions, or difficult terrain, all of which can affect data collection efforts.

Before heading into the field, it is recommended that you carefully configure and test your data collection form. Testing helps ensure that required fields, data validation rules, GPS settings, and attachments are functioning as expected. Taking the time to prepare and validate your workflow can reduce errors, improve data quality, and create a smoother field experience for both individual collectors and project teams.
